# Controlled Pretexting Package — Delivery Scenario

**Assessment:** SDS-AC-001 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 22 October 2026

**FICTIONAL ACADEMIC EXERCISE — NO HARDWARE OR LIVE TESTING.** Sentinel Defense Systems is the assignment's fictional defense contractor. No real contractor, employee, credential, database, door or biometric system was accessed. `G-` denotes supplied descriptions, `A-` explicit assumptions and `SIM-` invented tabletop events. Procedures below describe only an owner-controlled training setup; they are not test results or permission to act on real facilities.

## 1. Scenario, backstory and authority

Chosen option: **Delivery**. The participant plays **Robin Hale**, a courier for the invented **Atlas Office Supplies**, bringing an empty carton representing a stationery sample for **Jordan Ellis**, the fictional Sentinel office-services host. Delivery reference **TRAIN-D01**, scheduled 10:00 at training reception R-01. The purpose is to assess visitor verification, host callback, badge restrictions and escort handover—not obtain sensitive material.

This is a scripted role-play with nominated participants, an informed controller and an approved training area. The employer, booking, names and paperwork are fictional; they are not claims about a real supplier. No real driver's license, professional credential, employee badge, invoice or government document is forged. Any actual identification used in a real authorized exercise must be genuine.

## 3. Word-for-word opening conversation

**Participant:** “Good morning. I'm Robin from Atlas Office Supplies. I have a stationery sample delivery for Jordan Ellis, reference TRAIN-D01. Could you check the appointment through your normal process, please?”

**Receptionist:** “Do you have identification and an appointment confirmation?”

**Participant:** “I can provide the identification permitted for this exercise and the delivery note. Please contact Jordan using your own directory. I'll wait here until you confirm.”

**Receptionist:** “Your host is not available yet.”

**Participant:** “That's fine. Is there an approved place to wait or leave the carton? I won't enter the office without your authorization.”

**Receptionist:** “Your host has confirmed. Please wear this visitor badge and wait for your escort.”

**Participant:** “Thank you. Please explain where the badge is valid and who will escort me.”

These lines test the ordinary verification path. They do not ask reception to skip checks, trust a phone number on the note, release a sensitive door or accept a claim of urgency.

## 4. Movement plan and documentation

1. Controller checks scope and stop phrase at the public training staging point SP-01.
2. Participant enters reception R-01 only through the approved normal entrance.
3. Remain at the visitor waiting line while identification, appointment and host callback are processed.
4. Accept only a receptionist-issued test badge. Record its visual distinction, expiry and stated door permissions without photographing real personal information.
5. Move to visitor meeting room V-01 only after the nominated escort accepts responsibility. If the controller has separately authorized a training corridor T-01, use that route with the escort; otherwise it is excluded.
6. Observe only verification, badge/escort process and access-control response. Do not open drawers, record faces/screens, attach devices or collect confidential information.
7. Return with escort to R-01, return the badge, reconcile the empty carton and check out with the controller.

Server rooms, laboratories, classified spaces, security consoles, actual staff-only offices and all unapproved rooms are excluded. A missing escort is a result to record from the approved location, not permission to roam.

## 5. Challenge and fallback responses

Fallbacks mean safe process responses, not new deceptive identities.

| Challenge | Exact response / action |
| --- | --- |
| “Who authorized you?” | “Please verify the appointment with Jordan through your directory. I'll stay here while you check.” |
| “You're not on the visitor list.” | “Then I won't enter. Please contact the exercise controller to resolve the booking.” |
| Escort leaves or route is unclear | “I'll wait at the approved stop point until the escort/controller confirms the route.” Stop movement |
| Staff/security appear concerned | “I'll stop here. Please contact the exercise controller for verification.” Do not argue or continue |
| Controller calls stop / real authority intervenes | “This is an authorized training exercise. I'm stopping and will cooperate with verification.” In a real engagement, present genuine ID and the executed authorization; a fictional document is not valid permission |

## 6. Success criteria, counter-detection and clean exit

A complete assessment records whether reception independently verified the host, issued a properly limited badge, maintained escort responsibility and recorded checkout. If staff deny access correctly, **the control succeeds and the assessment still produces a valid outcome**. A weakness is established only by the controller's documented simulated/observed action within scope; no finding is invented in advance.

Abort for any challenge requiring verification, boundary uncertainty, distress, an unexpected person in the training area, communications loss, real alarm or a controller/security stop. Never touch fire release or REX controls to continue. Leave by the normal authorized route when directed; return the badge/carton and debrief. Do not flee, hide, change the story or try another entrance after a stop.
