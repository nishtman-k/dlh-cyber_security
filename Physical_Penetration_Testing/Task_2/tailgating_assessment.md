# Tailgating, Piggybacking and Vestibule Assessment Plan

**Assessment:** SDS-AC-001 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 22 October 2026

**FICTIONAL ACADEMIC EXERCISE — NO HARDWARE OR LIVE TESTING.** Sentinel Defense Systems is the assignment's fictional defense contractor. No real contractor, employee, credential, database, door or biometric system was accessed. `G-` denotes supplied descriptions, `A-` explicit assumptions and `SIM-` invented tabletop events. Procedures below describe only an owner-controlled training setup; they are not test results or permission to act on real facilities.

## Purpose and scope

Evaluate one-person/one-authorization passage and staff response, using a tabletop or controlled training doorway only. Tailgating is following without knowing assistance; piggybacking is entry with assistance. Terminology varies, so the test plan defines it explicitly.

All times, door IDs, traffic and staff arrangements below are **A-01 modelling assumptions**, not supplied observations. No real attempt or detection result is claimed. The method is not approved for classified areas, operational security zones or emergency exits.

## Three opportunities and safe test design

| ID | Modelled opportunity | Control hypothesis / proposed validation | Proof and success/failure record | Counter-detection / stop rule | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TG-01 | Training employee door D-01, assumed 08:30 group arrival | Can a nominated second participant be admitted without their own authorization decision? Stage a controller-supervised group-arrival role-play, with stop line inside the training boundary | Record individual badge presentation, challenge and controller log. Access denied or safely challenged means the control worked. A modelled unverified follower past the stop line is a test finding, not full compromise | Staff challenge, visible concern, unexpected person, door obstruction or a controller stop ends the exercise immediately | Reinforce individual passage and accessible challenge/reporting; assess appropriate occupancy controls |
| TG-02 | Training visitor-to-office boundary D-02, assumed 10:00 host handover | Does escort responsibility transfer explicitly? Use the approved delivery role-play; assess whether the participant is held at reception until a named escort accepts | Record host verification, badge limits and escort handover. Never continue unescorted beyond the training line | Any uncertainty about host, route, authority or occupant consent means wait and contact controller; no invented second story | Define named escort ownership and restricted visitor-door permissions |
| TG-03 | Training employee-return doorway D-03, assumed 12:30 group return | Is credential policy consistent when several nominated participants return together? Use the same lab identity/authorization rules rather than distracting staff | Record whether each participant's entitlement is checked. Social familiarity or dress must not substitute for authorization | Challenge or concern prompts a normal safe stop and verification, not confidence escalation or movement to another entrance | Consistent return-entry checks, safe signage and no exception based only on group membership |

An opportunity is a hypothesis, not an instruction to time an actual intrusion. No props are used to block a person's view or hands, no touching/pushing, and no door is held in a way that compromises safety. Use only a separate, approved test boundary with a controller present.

## Vestibule / mantrap / airlock assessment

Modelled flow: public area → Door 1 authentication → camera-monitored vestibule → Door 2 authentication/biometric → restricted area. A secure vestibule normally interlocks doors, but safety and emergency-release design can create permitted exceptions.

| Dimension | Tabletop assessment | Evidence / expected control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-passback | Review duplicate-use and missing exit-event handling with test identities | Controller policy and exceptions; anti-passback tracks credential state and is not automatically one-person detection |
| Occupancy / weight sensors | Review occupancy logic, accessibility, calibration and exception handling | Authorized commissioning records; never overload, obstruct or manipulate an occupied vestibule |
| Camera coverage | Review a model drawing and authorized image-quality/response checks | Verified field of view, overlap and response; camera presence alone is insufficient |
| Intercom | Role-play a request with the controller; verify identity through a trusted channel | Guard follows verification/exception policy, not a caller's asserted authority |
| Request-to-exit (REX) | Review configuration and commissioning records | Safe egress preserved; no external triggering or release-manipulation procedure is included |
| Fire release | Review safety-approved design and test records with the responsible specialist | No alarm activation, fire-release test or life-safety bypass in this exercise; emergency egress must remain operational |
